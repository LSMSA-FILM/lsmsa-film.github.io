# What is livestreaming used for?

At LSMSA, livestreaming is used to allow family and friends of LSMSA students to watch an event occurring at LSMSA, especially for those who otherwise would not be able to make it to that in-person event. This can include large-scale school assemblies (like graduation, ring ceremonies, matriculation ceremonies, community meetings, etc.) or student-run events (like music recitals, talent shows, athletics events, and club commissions).

{: .note-title}

> Terminology
>
> The word "livestreaming" is used instead of "broadcasting" because livestreaming typically refers to streaming over the Internet, while broadcasting refers to streaming over the radio and TV.

In general, LSMSA livestreams fall into one of the following types of events:

1. **[School Assemblies]({% link docs/streaming-for-assemblies/index.md %})** - Graduation, ring ceremonies, matriculation ceremonies, convocations, etc.
2. **[Sports]({% link docs/streaming-for-sports/index.md %})** - SLAMT, home games during a sport's season, etc.
   More information about how to livestream for that type of event is available on their corresponding page in this wiki.

# Where are livestreams sent to?

As of the end of 2025, livestreams at LSMSA are typically sent to one of the three following destinations:

1. **[LSMSA Official Vimeo Channel](https://vimeo.com/lsmsa)** - For large, important events typically involving the entire student body, including graduation, ring ceremonies, matriculation ceremonies, and Convocations (community meetings).
2. **[LSMSA Official YouTube Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@LSMSAvids)** - Rarely used
3. **[LSMSA Film Club YouTube Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@lsmsastudentfilm)** - For student-run events from clubs and the music department, including music recitals, talent shows, athletics events, and club commissions.

{: .note}

> The goal is that streams that are sent to the LSMSA Vimeo channel will be streamed concurrently to the LSMSA YouTube channel. This is because, while Vimeo has no ads and is objectively better, people are more familiar with and comfortable using YouTube.

There were some past destinations that livestreams used to be sent to:

- **[LSMSA Athletics Instagram Page](https://www.instagram.com/lsmsaathletics/reels/)** - Was for athletics events. This has been phased out in favor of the LSMSA Film Club YouTube channel, as Instagram makes it difficult to livestream for the following reasons[^1]:
  - Instagram only allows vertical livestreams of resolution 1080 by 1920 px.
  - You can only stream directly from a camera, so no post production can be done
  - There is a maximum time limit of 4 hours

# How do you livestream?

At LSMSA, there are several methods that enables one to livestream to any one (or multiple) of the aforementioned destinations. Each of them have their own pros and cons, which are listed in [[Description of the Different Methods of Livestreaming]]. The primary methods for livestreaming are as follows:

1. **LSMSA's TriCaster (TC Mini X) system** - Allows for advanced livestreaming and professional-looking post-production work to be done to the livestream. The "TriCaster" allows for on-screen graphics, changing camera angles, transitions, and much more. Steep learning curve.
2. **OBS Studio on a student's personal computer** - Allows for advanced livestreaming. With some work and the addition of several plugins (listed in [[Plugins for OBS Studio]]), you can get a professional look that approaches that of the TriCaster. Requires a lot of work to get something that looks good; you can use one of the pre-made scene collections in [[Scene Collections for OBS Studio]].
3. **A livestream broadcaster box** - Lets you stream directly from a camera output. Doesn't allow for post-production, but is the simplest to set up and can be left alone during the event. Because login information can be stored on the device, these devices are portable and can be easily plugged into an existing camera setup (for example, the setup at NSU's A.A. Fredericks Hall).
